The Agreement Loop

How a chatbot can help a shaky belief grow, drawn as a machine you can adjust

1. What the term means

“AI psychosis” is a popular label, not a diagnosis. It is used for cases where a person's delusional beliefs seem to form or deepen during long chatbot conversations. A 2026 arXiv paper, “An Echo Chamber of One”, asks whether it should be treated as a distinct clinical entity at all, and notes that current understanding rests largely on media-reported cases and individual case reports. Some clinicians prefer narrower wording such as “AI-associated delusions”, since the reported cases mostly involve delusions rather than the full range of psychotic symptoms.

The behaviour most often blamed is sycophancy: a model agreeing with, flattering, or building on whatever the user says, even when it is wrong.

Open question: how much the chatbot causes versus amplifies. Clinicians quoted in coverage lean toward amplification of existing vulnerability. Treat everything below as a teaching sketch, not a prediction.

4. What the toy shows

  • Agreement alone is not enough: with plenty of outside contact, the loop stalls.
  • Time matters because each pass compounds the last.
  • Isolation removes the brake, so the same chatbot behaves very differently for different people.
  • Safeguards work by cutting the loop at a different point than people do.

Caveat. This is a hand-built model with invented coefficients. It illustrates a proposed mechanism; it has not been fitted to data. If you or someone you know is distressed or holding beliefs that feel out of step with reality, talking to a clinician or someone you trust beats any chatbot.
